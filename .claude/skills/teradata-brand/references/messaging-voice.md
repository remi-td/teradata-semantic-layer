# Teradata Messaging and Voice Reference

## Overview
This document provides comprehensive guidelines for Teradata's brand voice, messaging framework, and content creation principles. Use this to ensure all written content reflects Teradata's brand personality and communicates effectively with target audiences.

## Brand Strategy Foundation

### Brand Narrative
Teradata empowers organizations to be business confident through trusted data and analytics. We help businesses:
- Prepare for anything with reliable, integrated data
- Make breakthrough decisions with AI-powered insights
- Drive positive impact with measurable business outcomes

**Core belief**: When data is trusted and insights are clear, businesses can act with confidence and transform their organizations.

### Brand Foundation Elements

#### Brand Promise
"Turn data into confident decisions that drive real business impact"

#### Brand Essence
Business confidence through data mastery

#### Brand Position
The trusted leader in enterprise data and analytics that enables confident decision-making at scale

## Brand Personality

Teradata's brand personality consists of four interconnected traits that should be reflected in all communications:

### 1. Courageous
**What it means**:
- Bold and decisive in approach
- Willing to challenge industry norms
- Not afraid to tackle complex problems
- Forward-thinking and pioneering

**How to express it**:
- Use strong, declarative statements
- Challenge conventional thinking
- Propose innovative solutions
- Lead conversations, don't follow
- Take clear positions on industry issues

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Traditional approaches to data can't keep pace with modern business"
- ✓ "It's time to rethink how we approach analytics"
- ✗ "Some companies are starting to consider new data approaches"
- ✗ "You might want to think about improving your analytics"

### 2. Confident
**What it means**:
- Self-assured without arrogance
- Expert knowledge backed by experience
- Authoritative yet approachable
- Certain about capabilities and value

**How to express it**:
- State facts clearly and directly
- Avoid hedging language ("we believe," "we think")
- Back claims with evidence
- Use active voice consistently
- Express certainty appropriately

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Teradata delivers enterprise-grade performance at scale"
- ✓ "Our platform handles the most demanding workloads"
- ✗ "We believe Teradata can help with performance"
- ✗ "Teradata might be a good option for your needs"

### 3. Dynamic
**What it means**:
- Energetic and forward-moving
- Innovative and adaptive
- Quick to evolve with market needs
- Driving industry progress

**How to express it**:
- Use active, strong verbs
- Focus on action and momentum
- Highlight innovation and evolution
- Emphasize speed and agility
- Show continuous improvement

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Transform data into action"
- ✓ "Accelerate time to insight"
- ✗ "Data can be used to inform decisions"
- ✗ "Insights are available when needed"

### 4. Compassionate
**What it means**:
- Empathetic to customer challenges
- Human-centered approach
- Collaborative partnership mindset
- Genuinely care about customer success

**How to express it**:
- Acknowledge real business challenges
- Use inclusive language ("we," "together")
- Focus on customer benefits, not just features
- Show understanding of context
- Emphasize partnership and support

**Examples**:
- ✓ "We understand the pressure to deliver results quickly"
- ✓ "Together, we'll navigate your data challenges"
- ✗ "Our product has these features"
- ✗ "You need to implement these capabilities"

## Voice Principles

Three core principles guide all Teradata communications:

### 1. Speak with Certainty

**Principle**: Be confident and decisive. Avoid hedging language that undermines authority.

**Avoid**:
- "We believe," "We think," "In our opinion"
- "Might," "Could," "Possibly," "Perhaps"
- "Try to," "Aim to," "Hope to"
- Passive constructions that diffuse responsibility

**Prefer**:
- "We deliver," "We enable," "We provide"
- "Will," "Can," "Does"
- Direct action verbs
- Active voice that shows agency

**Before/After Examples**:

❌ "We believe Teradata can help improve your analytics capabilities"
✅ "Teradata improves your analytics capabilities"

❌ "Our solution might be able to reduce costs"
✅ "Our solution reduces costs by up to 40%"

❌ "We try to provide excellent customer support"
✅ "We provide 24/7 enterprise support"

### 2. Speak Mindfully

**Principle**: Be clear and purposeful. Respect the audience's time by eliminating unnecessary words and getting to the point.

**Avoid**:
- Jargon without context
- Overly complex sentence structures
- Redundant phrases
- Filler words and fluff
- Buzzwords that lack meaning

**Prefer**:
- Clear, direct language
- Concise explanations
- Defined technical terms
- Purposeful word choices
- Concrete examples

**Before/After Examples**:

❌ "Leveraging synergies across the data ecosystem to drive digital transformation"
✅ "Connect all your data to make better decisions"

❌ "Our next-generation, AI-powered, cloud-native platform solution"
✅ "Our AI-powered cloud platform"

❌ "At this point in time, we are able to facilitate the implementation"
✅ "We can implement this now"

### 3. Speak Actively

**Principle**: Use active voice and strong verbs. Show action, momentum, and clear ownership.

**Active Voice Structure**: Subject → Verb → Object
- "Teradata enables confident decisions" (not "Confident decisions are enabled by Teradata")
- "Our team solved the problem" (not "The problem was solved by our team")

**Strong Verb Choices**:
- **Weak**: Make, do, have, get, use
- **Strong**: Transform, accelerate, drive, enable, empower, optimize, streamline

**Before/After Examples**:

❌ "Insights are delivered by our platform"
✅ "Our platform delivers insights"

❌ "Data quality issues are being addressed"
✅ "We address data quality issues"

❌ "Better decisions can be made with our analytics"
✅ "Make better decisions with our analytics"

## Messaging Framework

### North Star Message
**"Be business confident"**

This is Teradata's overarching message that should inform all communications. It encapsulates what Teradata enables: the confidence to make bold decisions backed by trusted data.

### Three Brand Pillars

Every piece of content should connect to at least one of these three pillars:

#### Pillar 1: Be Ready
**Core message**: "Prepare for anything with trusted, reliable data"

**Key themes**:
- Data readiness and quality
- Integration and accessibility
- Trust and reliability
- Future-proofing
- Preparedness for any scenario

**Sample messages**:
- "Keep your data ready for any question"
- "Integrate all your data sources seamlessly"
- "Trust your data to make critical decisions"
- "Prepare your organization for what's next"

**When to emphasize**: Data management, integration, quality, governance topics

#### Pillar 2: Make Breakthrough Decisions
**Core message**: "Transform data into actionable insights with AI/ML"

**Key themes**:
- AI and machine learning capabilities
- Advanced analytics
- Speed to insight
- Intelligence and discovery
- Competitive advantage

**Sample messages**:
- "Turn data into breakthrough insights"
- "Discover opportunities hidden in your data"
- "Make decisions faster with AI-powered analytics"
- "Gain competitive advantage through intelligence"

**When to emphasize**: Analytics, AI/ML, business intelligence, decision-making topics

#### Pillar 3: Drive Positive Impact
**Core message**: "Create measurable business outcomes that matter"

**Key themes**:
- Business results and ROI
- Measurable outcomes
- Positive change
- Customer success
- Real-world impact

**Sample messages**:
- "Deliver results that transform your business"
- "Create measurable impact across your organization"
- "Drive growth with data-driven decisions"
- "Achieve outcomes that matter"

**When to emphasize**: Business value, ROI, case studies, outcomes

### Messaging Hierarchy

When creating content, structure messages from broad to specific:

1. **North Star** (overarching): Be business confident
2. **Pillar** (strategic theme): One of the three pillars
3. **Key Message** (specific claim): Concrete value proposition
4. **Proof Point** (supporting evidence): Data, customer example, technical detail

**Example**:
1. North Star: "Be business confident"
2. Pillar: "Make breakthrough decisions"
3. Key Message: "Discover hidden opportunities with AI-powered analytics"
4. Proof Point: "Leading retailers use Teradata to identify cross-sell opportunities, increasing revenue by 15%"

## Content Creation Guidelines

### Writing for Different Audiences

#### C-Suite Executives
**Focus**: Business outcomes, strategic advantage, ROI
**Tone**: Confident, strategic, results-oriented
**Language**: Business terms, high-level concepts, financial impact
**Length**: Brief, scannable, respect their time
**Example**: "Accelerate decision-making and drive 20% revenue growth with enterprise-grade analytics"

#### IT Leaders & Data Teams
**Focus**: Technical capabilities, integration, performance, scalability
**Tone**: Expert, authoritative, technically accurate
**Language**: Technical terms (but still clear), architecture, specifications
**Length**: More detail acceptable, comprehensive explanations
**Example**: "Deploy a unified analytics platform that handles 10PB+ workloads with consistent sub-second query performance"

#### Business Analysts & Users
**Focus**: Usability, insights, day-to-day value, productivity
**Tone**: Practical, helpful, empowering
**Language**: Clear and accessible, avoid heavy technical jargon
**Length**: Moderate, focus on how-to and benefits
**Example**: "Access all your data in one place and create dashboards in minutes, not days"

#### Technical Practitioners (Data Scientists, Analysts)
**Focus**: Tools, capabilities, methodology, flexibility
**Tone**: Collaborative, technically proficient, innovative
**Language**: Technical but accessible, methodology-focused
**Length**: Detailed when relevant, examples appreciated
**Example**: "Build and deploy machine learning models using Python, R, or SQL with native in-database processing"

### Content Types and Approaches

#### Headlines
**Purpose**: Grab attention and communicate key benefit
**Best practices**:
- Lead with benefit or outcome
- Use sentence case (not title case)
- Keep to one line when possible
- Use strong, active verbs
- Be specific, not generic

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Turn data into confident decisions"
- ✓ "Scale analytics without limits"
- ✗ "The Power of Data Analytics"
- ✗ "Innovative Solutions for Modern Business"

#### Subheadings
**Purpose**: Provide context and lead into content
**Best practices**:
- Support and expand on headline
- Can be slightly longer (2 lines max)
- Provide necessary context
- Maintain parallel structure in lists

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Integrate, analyze, and activate data at enterprise scale"
- ✓ "Get the insights you need to make breakthrough decisions"

#### Body Copy
**Purpose**: Provide detail, evidence, and complete the story
**Best practices**:
- Start with most important information
- Use short paragraphs (3-4 sentences max)
- Include specific examples and data
- End paragraphs with forward momentum
- Transition smoothly between ideas

**Structure**:
1. Lead with benefit or key claim
2. Provide supporting evidence
3. Give concrete example or data point
4. Connect to next idea or CTA

#### Calls-to-Action (CTAs)
**Purpose**: Drive specific action
**Best practices**:
- Use strong action verbs
- Be specific about what happens next
- Create urgency or value
- Keep button text short (2-4 words)

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Start your free trial"
- ✓ "See how it works"
- ✓ "Talk to an expert"
- ✗ "Click here"
- ✗ "Learn more" (too vague)
- ✗ "Submit" (no value communicated)

### Writing Strong Proof Points

Proof points substantiate claims and build credibility. Strong proof points include:

**Quantitative Data**:
- "Reduce query time by 10x"
- "Process 100+ petabytes of data"
- "Achieve 99.99% uptime"

**Customer Evidence**:
- "Global bank processes 50 billion transactions daily"
- "Leading retailer increased sales by 15% using predictive analytics"

**Industry Recognition**:
- "Named a Leader in Gartner Magic Quadrant for 15 consecutive years"
- "Trusted by 8 of the top 10 Fortune 500 companies"

**Technical Capabilities**:
- "Native support for 20+ analytic languages and frameworks"
- "Seamless integration with major cloud providers"

### Storytelling Framework

Use this narrative structure for case studies, customer stories, and longer content:

1. **Challenge**: What problem did the customer face?
2. **Solution**: How did Teradata address it?
3. **Implementation**: What was the approach?
4. **Results**: What measurable outcomes were achieved?
5. **Future**: What's possible next?

**Example**:
"A global retailer struggled with fragmented data across 50+ systems (Challenge). They implemented Teradata VantageCloud to unify their data and enable AI-powered analytics (Solution). Working with our team, they deployed a customer 360 platform in just 12 weeks (Implementation). The result: 15% increase in cross-sell conversion and $50M additional revenue (Results). Now they're expanding into predictive inventory management (Future)."

## Messaging for Specific Scenarios

### Product Launches
**Focus**: Innovation, capability, business value
**Structure**:
1. Lead with customer benefit
2. Explain what's new and why it matters
3. Technical capabilities (brief)
4. Customer/industry impact
5. Availability and CTA

**Tone**: Confident, exciting, forward-looking

### Customer Success Stories
**Focus**: Real results, relatable challenges, authentic voice
**Structure**:
1. Customer intro (industry, challenge)
2. Solution approach
3. Measurable results
4. Customer quote
5. Broader implications

**Tone**: Authentic, results-focused, customer-centric

### Thought Leadership
**Focus**: Industry perspective, expertise, forward vision
**Structure**:
1. Industry trend or challenge
2. Teradata perspective
3. Implications for businesses
4. Recommended approach
5. How Teradata enables success

**Tone**: Authoritative, insightful, strategic

### Competitive Positioning
**Focus**: Differentiation, unique value, why Teradata
**Structure**:
1. Market challenge or consideration
2. Various approaches (generic)
3. Teradata's differentiated approach
4. Benefits of this approach
5. Results customers achieve

**Tone**: Confident but not arrogant, factual, customer-focused

**Important**: Never name competitors directly. Focus on capabilities and approaches, not brands.

## Language and Terminology

### Technical Terms
When using technical terminology:
- **Define on first use** if audience may not know it
- **Use consistently** once introduced
- **Avoid acronym overload** in a single sentence
- **Provide context** for why it matters

### Industry Buzzwords
Approach with caution:
- **Acceptable** (if defined): AI/ML, digital transformation, cloud-native, data fabric
- **Use sparingly**: Synergy, leverage, ecosystem, next-generation
- **Avoid**: Paradigm shift, revolutionary, game-changer (overused, vague)

### Teradata-Specific Terms
Always use these correctly (see `style-terminology.md` for complete list):
- **Teradata VantageCloud** (not "Vantage Cloud")
- **ClearScape Analytics** (capital S)
- **QueryGrid** (one word, capital Q and G)
- **AI/ML** (with hyphen and slash)
