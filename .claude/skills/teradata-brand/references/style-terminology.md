# Teradata Style Guide and Terminology Reference

## Overview
This comprehensive style guide provides rules for grammar, punctuation, formatting, and terminology specific to Teradata communications. Use this reference to ensure consistency and correctness across all content.

## General Mechanics

### Capitalization

#### Sentence Case (Preferred)
Use sentence case for headlines, subheads, and titles:
- ✓ "Transform your data into actionable insights"
- ✗ "Transform Your Data Into Actionable Insights"

**Exception**: Proper nouns and product names always capitalize appropriately

#### Title Case (Avoid)
Do not use title case for headlines in marketing materials:
- ✗ "The Future of Enterprise Analytics"
- ✓ "The future of enterprise analytics"

#### Product Names
Capitalize product names as specified:
- Teradata Vantage
- Teradata VantageCloud
- ClearScape Analytics (note: capital S in Scape)
- QueryGrid (one word, two capitals)

#### Generic Terms
Do not capitalize generic technology terms:
- data warehouse (not Data Warehouse)
- cloud computing (not Cloud Computing)
- artificial intelligence (not Artificial Intelligence)

**Exception**: When part of a proper product name (e.g., "Teradata Cloud Data Platform")

### Numbers

#### Spell Out vs. Numerals

**Spell out**:
- One through nine (e.g., "five customers," "eight features")
- Numbers at beginning of sentence: "Forty-five percent of respondents..."
- Rounded large numbers: "five million," "two billion"

**Use numerals**:
- 10 and above (e.g., "15 customers," "42 features")
- Percentages: "25% improvement" (always use % symbol with numeral)
- Money: "$5 million," "$2.5 billion"
- Technical specifications: "256GB RAM," "10 petabytes"
- Ages: "5 years old," "10-year-old company"
- Dates: "March 15, 2025"
- Times: "9:00 am" or "3:30 pm"

#### Large Numbers
- Use commas: 1,000 or 1,500,000
- Or use abbreviations: 1.5M, 2.5B
- Be consistent within a document
- Petabytes: "10PB" or "10 petabytes" (not "10 PB")

#### Percentages
- Use numeral + % symbol: "25% faster"
- Include space in spelled-out form: "25 percent"
- Round appropriately: "33%" not "33.333%"

### Dates and Times

#### Date Format
**Standard**: Month DD, YYYY
- March 15, 2026
- January 1, 2025

**Shortened** (when appropriate): MM/DD/YYYY
- 03/15/2026

**Avoid**: DD/MM/YYYY (confusing for US audience)

#### Time Format
**Standard**: 12-hour with am/pm (lowercase, no periods)
- 9:00 am
- 3:30 pm
- 11:45 pm

**Avoid**:
- 24-hour time (15:00) unless for technical/international audiences
- AM/PM (use lowercase am/pm)
- A.M./P.M. (no periods)

#### Time Zones
- Use abbreviations: EST, PST, CST, GMT, UTC
- Specify when relevant: "9:00 am EST"

### Acronyms and Abbreviations

#### First Use
Always define acronyms on first use in a document:
- "artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML)"
- "customer relationship management (CRM) system"

**Exception**: Widely known acronyms (FBI, CEO, URL)

#### Subsequent Uses
Use the acronym consistently after defining:
- First: "application programming interface (API)"
- Later: "The API enables..."

#### Plural Forms
Add "s" without apostrophe:
- APIs (not API's)
- CRMs (not CRM's)

### Lists and Bullets

#### Parallel Structure
Maintain consistent grammatical structure:

✓ **Good**:
- Integrate data from multiple sources
- Analyze patterns with AI/ML
- Deploy insights to production

✗ **Bad**:
- Integrate data from multiple sources
- AI/ML pattern analysis
- Deployment of insights

#### Punctuation in Lists

**If list items are complete sentences**:
- Start with capital letter
- End with period.
- Treat each as a sentence.

**If list items are fragments**:
- Start with lowercase (unless proper noun)
- No ending punctuation
- Keep concise

**If list items complete a lead-in sentence**:
- Start with lowercase
- End with semicolon;
- Except the last item, which ends with a period.

#### Numbered vs. Bulleted Lists

**Use numbered lists**:
- Sequential steps or processes
- Ranked items (top 5, etc.)
- When order matters

**Use bulleted lists**:
- Unordered items
- Features or benefits
- When order doesn't matter

### Punctuation

#### Commas
**Use commas**:
- In series: "data, analytics, and insights" (Oxford comma required)
- After introductory phrases: "After analyzing the data, we found..."
- To separate independent clauses: "We tested the solution, and it worked"

**Oxford comma required**: Always use serial comma before "and" in lists
- ✓ "scalable, secure, and reliable"
- ✗ "scalable, secure and reliable"

#### Hyphens and Dashes

**Hyphen (-)**: Compound modifiers
- cloud-native platform
- real-time analytics
- end-to-end solution
- on-premises deployment

**En dash (–)**: Ranges (option + hyphen on Mac)
- 2025–2026
- 9:00 am–5:00 pm
- pages 15–20

**Em dash (—)**: Parenthetical statements (option + shift + hyphen on Mac)
- "Teradata VantageCloud—our cloud-native platform—enables..."
- Use sparingly; prefer commas or parentheses

#### Apostrophes
**Possessives**:
- Singular: customer's data, business's needs
- Plural: customers' requirements, businesses' challenges

**Contractions** (use sparingly in formal content):
- don't, can't, won't (avoid in formal docs)
- Prefer: do not, cannot, will not

**Never use for plurals**:
- ✗ API's, 1990's, CEO's
- ✓ APIs, 1990s, CEOs

#### Quotation Marks
**Use for**:
- Direct quotes: The CEO said, "We exceeded our goals."
- Scare quotes sparingly: This "solution" didn't work.

**Punctuation placement** (US style):
- Commas and periods inside: "data," "analytics."
- Colons and semicolons outside: "data"; "analytics":
- Question marks depend on context

#### Colons
**Use after complete sentence**:
- "We offer three deployment options: cloud, hybrid, and on-premises."

**Don't use after incomplete sentence**:
- ✗ "Our products include: Vantage and VantageCloud"
- ✓ "Our products include Vantage and VantageCloud"

### Formatting

#### Bold
Use for emphasis sparingly:
- Key terms on first mention
- Important warnings or notes
- Headers and subheaders (per style guide)

#### Italics
Use for:
- Publication titles: *Harvard Business Review*
- Emphasis (sparingly)
- Latin phrases: *e.g.*, *i.e.*, *et al.*

#### ALL CAPS
Avoid except for:
- Acronyms: AI, ML, API, SQL
- File extensions: .CSV, .JSON, .XML

#### Underline
Avoid—reserved for hyperlinks in digital content

## Industry Terms and Technology

### AI and Machine Learning

#### AI/ML
- Use hyphen and slash: "AI/ML"
- Define on first use: "artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML)"
- Use consistently after definition

#### Artificial Intelligence
- Two words, lowercase (unless starting sentence)
- Can abbreviate to AI after first use

#### Machine Learning
- Two words, lowercase (unless starting sentence)
- Can abbreviate to ML after first use

#### Deep Learning
- Two words, lowercase
- No widely accepted abbreviation

#### Related Terms
- **neural network** (two words)
- **natural language processing** (NLP after first use)
- **computer vision** (two words)
- **predictive analytics** (two words)
- **generative AI** (not "GenAI" in formal writing)

## Grammar and Usage

### Active vs. Passive Voice

#### Prefer Active Voice
**Active**: Subject performs action
- ✓ "Teradata delivers insights"
- ✓ "Data scientists analyze patterns"
- ✓ "The platform processes queries"

**Passive**: Action performed on subject
- ✗ "Insights are delivered by Teradata"
- ✗ "Patterns are analyzed by data scientists"
- ✗ "Queries are processed by the platform"

**When passive is acceptable**:
- When actor is unknown or unimportant
- When emphasizing the action/result over the actor
- In technical documentation for objectivity

### Common Usage Issues

#### That vs. Which
- **That**: Restrictive clause (essential to meaning, no comma)
  - "The data that is verified is ready"
- **Which**: Non-restrictive clause (additional info, use comma)
  - "The data, which was verified yesterday, is ready"

#### Fewer vs. Less
- **Fewer**: Countable items
  - "fewer queries," "fewer customers"
- **Less**: Uncountable quantities
  - "less time," "less complexity," "less data" (data is singular mass noun)

#### May vs. Can
- **May**: Permission or possibility
  - "You may access the dashboard"
- **Can**: Ability
  - "The platform can process petabytes"

#### Ensure vs. Insure vs. Assure
- **Ensure**: Make certain (most common in tech writing)
  - "Ensure data quality"
- **Insure**: Insurance coverage
  - "Insure against data loss"
- **Assure**: Convince someone
  - "We assure you it's secure"

#### Affect vs. Effect
- **Affect**: Verb (to influence)
  - "Performance affects business outcomes"
- **Effect**: Noun (result), rarely verb (to cause)
  - "The effect was significant"
  - "Effect change" (formal, rare)

### Prepositions

#### Ending Sentences
**Modern style allows ending with prepositions** when natural:
- ✓ "This is the data we're looking for"
- ✗ "This is the data for which we're looking" (overly formal)

#### Common Preposition Pairings
- **different from** (not "different than")
- **comply with** (not "comply to")
- **comprised of** (avoid; use "composed of" or "consists of")
- **impacts** (avoid as verb; use "affects" or "has an impact on")

### Verb Tense

#### Consistency
Maintain consistent tense within sections:
- ✓ "The platform processes data and delivers insights"
- ✗ "The platform processes data and delivered insights"

#### Present Tense Preferred
For product descriptions and capabilities:
- ✓ "Vantage integrates data from multiple sources"
- Not: "Vantage will integrate..." or "Vantage integrated..."

## Accessibility and Inclusive Language

### Inclusive Writing

#### Gender-Neutral Language
**Avoid**:
- "he/she," "his/hers"
- "guys" (as group address)
- "man-hours" (use "work hours")
- "chairman" (use "chair" or "chairperson")

**Prefer**:
- "they/them" (singular acceptable)
- "everyone," "team," "colleagues"
- "workforce," "staff"
- Gender-neutral job titles

#### Examples:
- ✗ "Each user should configure his settings"
- ✓ "Each user should configure their settings"
- ✓ "Users should configure their settings" (plural preferred)

### Accessible Writing

#### Clear Language
- Use short, simple sentences when possible
- Define technical terms
- Avoid idiomatic expressions that don't translate well
- Be direct and concrete

#### Considerate Language
**Avoid**:
- "Simple," "easy," "just" (dismissive of challenges)
- "Obviously," "clearly," "simply" (implies reader should know)
- Ableist terms: "blind spot," "lame," "crazy"

**Prefer**:
- "Straightforward," "efficient"
- Direct explanation without judgment
- Literal descriptions

## Trademarks and Legal

### Trademark Symbols
- ® : Registered trademark
- ™ : Unregistered trademark

**Usage**:
- Use on first mention in document
- Not needed in every instance
- Follow Teradata's official trademark list
- "Teradata®" on first mention

### Copyright
- © Teradata Corporation [YEAR]
- Include on all external materials
- Update year annually

### Third-Party Trademarks
**Acknowledge third-party trademarks**:
- "Amazon Web Services, AWS, and related marks are trademarks of Amazon.com, Inc."
- Include standard disclaimer when extensively mentioning partners

## Quick Reference Lists

### Commonly Misspelled Terms
- ✓ analytics (not analtics)
- ✓ occurred (not occured)
- ✓ separate (not seperate)
- ✓ database (not data base)
- ✓ on-premises (not on-premise)
- ✓ skillset or skill set (both acceptable, be consistent)

### Hyphenation Quick Guide
**Always hyphenate**:
- cloud-native (as adjective)
- on-premises
- real-time (as adjective)
- end-to-end
- self-service (as adjective)
- high-performance (as adjective)

**Never hyphenate**:
- data warehouse
- machine learning
- data center
- business intelligence

### Abbreviations to Avoid
**Don't abbreviate** (spell out):
- information (not "info")
- application (not "app" in formal writing)
- demonstration (not "demo" in formal writing)
- specifications (not "specs" in formal writing)
